ESG Strategy Report for Volkswagen Group: Post-Emission Scandal Recovery

By Mubarak Ibrahim · Corporate report · Crisis management & sustainability

Analysis of the Emission Scandal

The “Dieselgate” scandal emerged in 2015 when Volkswagen (VW) was discovered to have installed defeat devices in millions of its diesel vehicles. The devices reduced nitrogen oxide emission to pass the emission tests under test conditions. Whereas the vehicles emitted pollutants above regulatory limits in real-world driving.

The key events happened when the U.S. Environmental Protection Agency (EPA) issued a violation notice to Volkswagen, beginning their investigation that revealed that around 500,000 vehicles in the US and over 10.5 million vehicles globally were fitted with this defeat device. Volkswagen admitted guilt which resulted in fines, recalls, and settlements totaling tens of billions of dollars.

Excess emission of nitrogen oxide contributes to air pollution causing environmental harm like acid rain and also respiratory complications. This action significantly damaged the Volkswagen brand as an environmentally friendly automaker, resulting in a loss of consumer trust with declined sales and stock prices shredding the shareholder confidence in the company. Coupled with the legal action and fines, the company was financially strained.

Crisis Management Plan

Rebuilding trust, commitment to sustainability, and ethics in Volkswagen, an effective crisis management strategy must be put into action to align with each key stakeholder. Majorly there are internal that consist of the employees, shareholders, and the management and external stakeholders like the customers, regulators, environmental groups, local communities, and the authorities.

Immediate actions are needed to control the situation, the first line of action will be a public apology and accountability admitting to the wrongdoing and explaining corrective measures clearly. Before the court has its verdict, recalls and compensation are initiated by the company to ensure all affected vehicles are off the market and compensation for the affected customer to show concern, retain trust, and reduce costs from the final verdict. The last action to look into will be the management structure, appointing new leadership that will restore the company's credibility and sustainability.

Communication strategies will be proactive engagement to communicate the progress transparently, dropping regular updates through media channels. Another strategy will have stakeholder dialogues like hosting open forums with the customers, and environmentally inclined NGOs to demonstrate accountability. Creating an internal department committing to sustainable innovation and transitioning to cleaner technologies like electric vehicles.

The Triple Bottom Line Framework

A TBL framework ensures Volkswagen recovery aligns with sustainable development goals by focusing on the people, planet, and profit, which are also known as environmental responsibility, social responsibility, and economic sustainability.

Environmental responsibility involves accelerating the shift from fuel-consuming vehicles to electric and hybrid automobiles. Investing in a carbon-neutral manufacturing process and adopting emission reduction solutions. To offset the nitrogen oxide emission damages, participation in environmental restoration projects like reforestation is encouraged.

Social responsibility by strengthening corporate ethics and compliance frameworks to improve transparency in the company. Focusing more on employees' diversity and welfare to encourage an ethical workplace culture. Promoting environmental awareness by launching community outreach programs.

Economic sustainability is inevitable if the company aligns with the framework and also implements transitioning towards long-term value creation by expanding more into the EV market like they have with the Porsche Taycan and focusing on green innovation. Initiatives like environmentally friendly incentives and extended warranties can help rebuild customer loyalty. Developing partnerships with sustainable supply chains to align with ESG principles will increase their integrity in the automobile market.

Practical Implementation Plan

The implementation plan will be looked into from the incidence to the long-term plan that will impact the company's future. The immediate recovery plan will be between the first 6 months to publicly disclose the findings of internal investigations. Implement changes in leadership to foster trust. This plan will involve the crisis management team, legal department, and executive leadership. The main challenge will be rebuilding public trust.

The midterm strategy will work the company through the 2 years, start by launching pilot programs for more electric vehicle production and phase out diesel models. In this plan, we develop a transparent non-financial reporting framework aligned with Global Reporting Initiative (GRI) standards to track ESG progress. This plan will be carried out by the operational team, marketing, and compliance department. Balancing financial recovery from the scandal with sustainable investment will be a major challenge that might be financially draining for the company.

After this, they will tend to focus more on the long-term sustainability vision, with the primary objective of positioning Volkswagen as a leader in the green mobility space with a comprehensive electric vehicle lineup. KPIs will be used to monitor the progress of environmental performance, employee satisfaction, and profit. Annual sustainability reports will be published to maintain the mitigated trust and transparency. The board of directors and sustainability teams will make this plan come to life and manage the evolving regulations and market competition for long-term sustainability.
